# Humanization Matrix

The pass below targets general English academic prose. It records observable
style risks and revisions; it does not claim or predict performance on any
commercial detector.

| Row ID | Manuscript Unit | AI Pattern Found | Detection Dim | Severity | Applied Change | Expected Effect | Teaching Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Abstract | An early draft used five similarly sized contribution sentences with repeated clause structure. | D1 | medium | Varied sentence length and function: problem, prior capability, system, study, and evidence boundary. | Reduces metronomic sentence rhythm while preserving the five-sentence contract. | Sentence variation should follow argumentative function, not random synonym replacement. |
| H2 | Introduction opening | Generic “recent advances” framing could have delayed the concrete problem. | D2 | medium | Opened with the leaderboard measurement problem and named optimizer, recipe, debugging, and search effort before discussing agents. | Replaces a stock field-summary opening with a falsifiable scene. | Concrete objects carry more authorial voice than broad progress claims. |
| H3 | Introduction prior-work paragraph | A list of agent systems risked reading as citation accumulation. | D3 | medium | Assigned each citation a specific capability, then used one contrast sentence to isolate the missing cross-method intervention. | Makes the paragraph causal rather than encyclopedic. | Citations should move the argument, not decorate it. |
| H4 | Contribution list | Repeated “we propose / we introduce / we show” phrasing could imply unsupported outcomes. | D4 | high | Used two noun-led contribution headings and stated that the C2 certified outcome is not claimed. | Removes promotional templates and aligns voice with evidence maturity. | Claim verbs must reflect whether evidence is established, audited, or planned. |
| H5 | Research-opportunity estimand | Dense notation could become formula-first prose detached from the scientific question. | D1 | medium | Introduced each symbol in a concrete study-cell sentence and followed the equations with a plain-language primary/secondary distinction. | Alternates formal and explanatory cadence. | Equations should remove ambiguity, then return the reader to the estimand. |
| H6 | Matched-opportunity boundary | Repeated fairness language risked slogan-like abstraction. | D2 | high | Replaced unqualified “fair/equal effort” with “matched launched-attempt opportunity” and enumerated what is not equalized. | Makes the intervention operational and less formulaic. | Precise negative boundaries often sound more credible than positive superlatives. |
| H7 | System section | A feature-by-feature inventory risked uniform subsection structure. | D3 | medium | Organized the method around one node’s research, execution, and evidence path, with failure semantics and threat limits inside the relevant layer. | Gives the section a process arc instead of a product catalogue. | Mechanisms are easier to follow when attached to a state transition. |
| H8 | Validation section | “We demonstrate” language could blur local tests, external audit records, and missing outcomes. | D4 | high | Split evidence by provenance and used “the cluster audit reports” for non-local records; added a blocker table for unestablished claims. | Prevents confident surface fluency from outrunning the evidence. | Source level belongs in the sentence, not only in a footnote. |
| H9 | Discussion | A generic limitations paragraph could become a long hedge list. | D2, D3 | medium | Grouped each threat with its specific failure mode and remedy: provenance, lineage, seeds, validation, and dependence. | Produces concrete argumentative variation rather than boilerplate caution. | A limitation is useful when it changes the required experiment. |
| H10 | Conclusion | Restating the abstract verbatim would create repetitive closure. | D1, D4 | medium | Closed with the artifact-level distinction between what autoMIL establishes now and what certification must establish later. | Avoids phrase reuse and ends on an actionable evidence standard. | Conclusions should resolve the paper’s contract, not replay its introduction. |
| H11 | Title | A slogan-like title could overpromise autonomous discovery. | D4 | medium | Named the system and its precise function: auditable source-level research for controlled model comparison. | Makes the scope visible before the abstract. | Functional titles age better than claims tied to one unfinished result. |
| H12 | Abstract sentence 1 | “Machine learning is rapidly advancing” was an available but empty opening pattern. | D2 | medium | Began with the measurement content of a leaderboard. | Increases specificity at the first sentence. | The opening should identify the object under dispute. |
| H13 | Abstract sentence 2 | Agent capability and evaluation control could be conflated in one promotional sentence. | D3 | medium | Contrasted source editing with attribution and comparability. | Creates a clear logical hinge. | Contrast is useful when both sides name concrete capabilities. |
| H14 | Abstract sentence 3 | A long feature list could obscure the integrated contract. | D1 | medium | Kept four linked mechanisms in one sentence because they form one operational chain. | Preserves density without fragmenting the claim. | Long sentences are justified when the clauses share one causal role. |
| H15 | Abstract sentence 4 | The benchmark could be described as a generic demonstration. | D4 | high | Named the cross-method ranking question and made within-method lift explanatory. | Prevents the empirical contribution from shrinking into per-arm optimization. | Primary and secondary estimands should appear together when confusion is likely. |
| H16 | Abstract sentence 5 | A conventional positive-result ending would fabricate maturity. | D4 | high | Ended with the exact available test/static evidence and named the missing campaign. | Signals an honest public-preprint state. | Evidence boundaries can be a substantive abstract result. |
| H17 | Introduction paragraph 1 | Hyperparameter sensitivity could be asserted without scale or provenance. | D3 | medium | Anchored the problem to tunability and optimizer-HPO benchmark studies. | Connects the motivation to established measurement literature. | A familiar phenomenon still needs the right methodological citation. |
| H18 | Introduction paragraph 2 | Sequential paper-name sentences could sound generated and repetitive. | D1 | medium | Combined three capability precedents, then separated trajectory/hidden-evaluation precedents. | Varies syntax according to evidence class. | Group references by argumentative job, not publication date. |
| H19 | Introduction paragraph 3 | “Our framework solves these challenges” would be too broad. | D4 | high | Described the experiment node and reconstruction path before naming the payoff. | Grounds the claim in an inspectable object. | Mechanism-first prose makes scope easier to audit. |
| H20 | Introduction contribution 1 | Eight mechanisms in a bullet risked becoming a product checklist. | D3 | medium | Bound them through the phrase “one executable contract.” | Keeps integration, not component priority, as the claim. | A system contribution needs a unifying invariant. |
| H21 | Introduction contribution 2 | “We benchmark MIL models” would not distinguish the study. | D2 | high | Specified native versus matched-search rankings and the role of paired lift. | Makes the scientific intervention legible. | Benchmark novelty lives in the comparison being estimated. |
| H22 | Estimand definitions | Symbols introduced in isolation can create compressed, impersonal prose. | D1 | medium | Embedded \(a\), \(c\), \(v\), \(p(v)\), and \(\Delta_v\) in one concrete study-cell narrative. | Improves continuity between notation and implementation. | Introduce notation in dependency order. |
| H23 | Estimand equations | Three equations in succession could interrupt the reading rhythm. | D1 | medium | Used two short setup sentences, equations, then one interpretive paragraph. | Restores a prose–math–prose cadence. | The paragraph after an equation should state what decision it changes. |
| H24 | Attempt semantics | “Failures count” might read as an arbitrary rule. | D3 | medium | Explained the incentive problem created by refunding failed launches. | Converts a rule into a methodological rationale. | Failure semantics matter when systems have different reliability. |
| H25 | Baseline definition | Native recipes could be mistaken for a claim of architecture-only fairness. | D4 | high | Explicitly stated which alternative question a shared recipe would answer. | Prevents an attractive but incorrect simplification. | Compare protocols by estimand, not by the word “standardized.” |
| H26 | Closest-prior paragraph | Defensive novelty prose can become vague or adversarial. | D2 | medium | Credited each non-novel ingredient and stated the residual measurement use. | Makes the delta direct without disparaging prior work. | The strongest novelty defense begins with accurate concessions. |
| H27 | Closest-prior table caption | Binary capability marks can imply that nuanced systems lack a feature entirely. | D3 | medium | Defined every axis and used “partial” where the match is not exact. | Reduces categorical overstatement. | Table legends carry methodological qualifications. |
| H28 | Research layer | Search-policy language could imply a new optimization algorithm. | D4 | high | Stated that human, heuristic, or coding-agent proposers can operate the same state. | Keeps the substrate distinct from policy performance. | Name replaceable components explicitly. |
| H29 | Keep/discard paragraph | “Validation firewall” could become security-flavored branding. | D2 | medium | Described which score controls expansion and why test feedback would invalidate inference. | Replaces metaphor with data flow. | Security-adjacent terms need operational definitions. |
| H30 | Execution reconstruction | A sequence of implementation verbs could sound like documentation pasted into a paper. | D1 | medium | Compressed path, digest, overlay, masking, logging, and collection around one queued-node lifecycle. | Retains technical detail with narrative direction. | Follow one object through the system instead of listing modules. |
| H31 | Orphan recovery | A low-level detail could appear as gratuitous engineering trivia. | D3 | low | Kept only the consequence: read-only status commands cannot mutate live state. | Ties the detail to evidence integrity. | Implementation details belong when they protect a paper claim. |
| H32 | Evidence layer | “Hard-refuses” could sound like an absolute architecture guarantee. | D4 | high | Limited refusal to consumer-declared protected paths and immediately stated the consumer obligation. | Prevents the enforcement verb from escaping its scope. | Every enforcement claim needs its declared input. |
| H33 | Certification paragraph | “One-time reveal” might be read as cryptographic secrecy. | D4 | high | Added the same-user threat-model exclusion in the paragraph and Figure 1 caption. | Aligns the security tone with the actual mechanism. | Repeat critical threat limits at the claim site. |
| H34 | Consumer portability | Plug-and-play language could imply no adaptation. | D2 | medium | Enumerated the four consumer-supplied contracts and limited evidence to one synthetic round trip. | Makes portability requirements tangible. | Reusability is an interface claim before it is a generality claim. |
| H35 | Cohort introduction | Pathology background could expand into a generic domain tutorial. | D3 | low | Kept only the properties that stress research-effort comparison: many patches, weak labels, modest cohorts, divergent recipes. | Preserves relevance for general ML readers. | Domain context should explain experimental difficulty. |

| H38 | Architecture-policy paragraph | Future-tense prose could hide an unresolved design choice. | D4 | high | Named both acceptable resolutions and the invalid post-result mixture. | Turns “future work” into a concrete decision gate. | State what would falsify the protocol. |

| H40 | Resource-ledger paragraph | Reproducibility could be reduced to source release. | D2 | medium | Listed provenance, trajectories, attempts, failures, compute, LLM cost, selections, and certification. | Broadens reproducibility to the research process. | Autonomous-research evidence includes unsuccessful work. |
