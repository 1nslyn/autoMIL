# Motivation Options After Research

All three options preserve the confirmed contribution order: C1 is the
framework contribution and C2 is the main empirical contribution. They differ
only in the opening problem and the paper's center of gravity.

## Option A — Rankings confound method quality with research effort

**Recommended.**

Published ML leaderboards usually compare methods after unequal amounts of
human recipe engineering. Coding agents make that hidden variable scalable but
do not by themselves make it measurable. We introduce autoMIL as an auditable
research-operations substrate for applying matched, attributable source-level
research effort to competing lineages in existing repositories. We then use it
to ask whether pathology-MIL rankings remain stable under a declared
equal-opportunity research protocol.

**Why it fits:** This makes C1 necessary rather than decorative and gives C2 a
clear scientific estimand. It also admits a null result: stable rankings would
validate the benchmark; changing rankings would expose recipe sensitivity.

**Main risk:** “Equal effort” must be defined narrowly and implemented
faithfully. Attempt counts, completed evaluations, compute, failures, and search
space difficulty cannot be conflated.

## Option B — Autonomous research lacks an evidence and operations contract

Autonomous ML systems can edit code and search candidate programs, but applying
them to existing multi-file repositories leaves a systems gap: experiments
must be reconstructable, parent-addressed, isolated, budgeted, recoverable, and
separated from final evaluation. autoMIL supplies this contract and autobench
demonstrates it through a controlled pathology-MIL study.

**Why it fits:** This is the strongest framework-first framing and most directly
supports C1 if the final C2 effect is small.

**Main risk:** Closest systems already contain many individual mechanisms.
Without strong end-to-end and cross-consumer evidence, reviewers may view the
contribution as integration rather than a new measurement capability.

## Option C — Pathology-MIL rankings may be recipe rankings

Computational-pathology MIL papers report cross-method leaderboards, yet
training recipes, tuning effort, encoders, and task-specific engineering differ
across methods. We build autoMIL to intervene on this confound and conduct a
controlled source-level audit across pathology cohorts and lineages.

**Why it fits:** It is concrete, domain-relevant, and places the strongest
potential empirical result early.

**Main risk:** It makes autoMIL look like supporting infrastructure rather than
the primary contribution, contrary to the confirmed contribution hierarchy. It
also depends most heavily on unfinished C2 results.

## Recommendation

Select **Option A**. It creates a single dependency:

> autoMIL is the measurement instrument; the pathology-MIL ranking audit is
> the scientific use of that instrument.

This also separates novelty correctly. The familiar ingredients in C1 become
the auditable contract required to estimate a less-studied object in C2:
cross-method ranking under matched, declared autonomous research opportunity.
